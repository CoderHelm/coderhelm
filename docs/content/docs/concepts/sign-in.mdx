title: Sign-in and two-factor
description: How people sign in to CoderHelm (password, Google, GitHub, Microsoft, a team's Okta), the one second factor each person has, how a team's admins require two-factor sign-in, and how an owner requires single sign-on.
---

People sign in to CoderHelm with an email and password, with Google, with GitHub, or with Microsoft (work, school and personal accounts). All of them lead to the same person (by email), on every team they are on. A team can also turn on sign-in with its own Okta, for its own members ([below](#okta-for-a-team)), and then require single sign-on with it or with the team's own Microsoft Entra tenant ([Require single sign-on](#require-single-sign-on)). This page is about the hosted CoderHelm at app.coderhelm.com; a self-hosted install has its own [sign-in page](/self-host/sign-in/).

Microsoft vouches for an email only when its token says the email's domain is verified (`xms_edov`), for work, school and personal accounts alike. Without that, sign-in stops and says so: sign in another way. CoderHelm never uses the account's display name (`preferred_username`) as its email.

## Okta for a team

A team's admins and owners can let people sign in with the team's own Okta: **Settings, Sign-in rules, Okta sign-in**.

1. In Okta, create an app integration: **OIDC - OpenID Connect**, **Web Application**, grant type Authorization Code. Its **Sign-in redirect URI** is `https://api.coderhelm.com/auth/okta/callback` (the section shows it with a **Copy** button). Assign the people or groups who use CoderHelm.
2. In CoderHelm, enter your Okta domain (such as `acme.okta.com`) or issuer, the app's **Client ID** and **Client secret**, and your work email domains. The secret is encrypted with AWS KMS and never shown again.
3. Choose **Test sign-in**: your Okta asks you to sign in, and CoderHelm makes every check a real sign-in makes. Nothing changes for anyone else.
4. Prove each email domain: add the TXT record the section shows (`_coderhelm-okta.<domain>`, value `coderhelm-okta=...`) in your DNS, then choose **Verify domains**. A domain can be proven by one team only, and Okta sign-in turns on only when every domain is verified.
5. When the test passed and the domains are verified, choose **Save and turn on**.

Then each member links their own Okta account: **Settings, Security, Link Okta**, from a session they started another way (password, Google, GitHub or Microsoft). **Continue with Okta** on the sign-in page asks for a work email and goes to the Okta of the team that proved that email's domain, and signs in only the member who linked exactly that Okta account. It never matches anyone by email, never accepts an invite and never makes an account or a team. Changing your Okta domain or removing Okta drops everyone's links. CoderHelm reads each domain's TXT record again when Okta is turned on and once a day at sign-in; a record that is gone releases the domain, and **Release** beside a domain does it by hand. An Okta session works for your team only: it can't switch to the person's other teams, change their password or second factor, link a GitHub account, or act as a CoderHelm admin. Someone on no other team can still set up a second factor from it. The person's own second factor is still asked for. Turning Okta sign-in on, off or removing it goes in the log under **Settings, Roles**.

## Require single sign-on

The owner can make the team's own identity provider the only way in: **Settings, Sign-in rules, Require single sign-on**, with either the team's **Okta** (above) or the team's own **Microsoft Entra tenant**. One at a time: to change it, turn the rule off and on again with the other.

**The team's own Microsoft tenant.** Sign in with Microsoft finds people by email, for any account. To require Microsoft, the owner records the team's tenant in **Settings, Sign-in rules, Microsoft Entra**: the tenant ID, and the email domains it signs in, each verified in DNS like an Okta domain. The owner runs **Test sign-in** through that tenant as themselves and turns it on within the hour. Each member links their own account in **Settings, Security, Microsoft**. A sign-in through the team's tenant lands only on the member who linked exactly that account; personal Microsoft accounts and other companies' tenants never count. A new tenant drops every link. Guests in the tenant (accounts another directory signs in) never count.

- **Only the owner** turns the rule on or off, and only when the provider is set up and on, every email domain of its setup is verified, and the owner's own session signed in with it, as the account they linked. The page says which of these is still missing.
- **There is no way around it.** While it is on, members sign in only with the team's provider. Password, Google, GitHub, any other Microsoft account and the other provider are refused for everyone, the owner included, with or without a second factor. Switching into the team from another team, and connecting an app from a session that didn't start with the provider, are refused too. The message names the team and shows only its provider's sign-in (the sign-in page shows no text taken from its address). If you are on other teams, only this team is affected.
- **Sessions that didn't start with the provider end at once, for good**, and the person lands on the team's sign-in. Turning the rule on records a cut-off that turning it off never clears, so those sessions, the short-lived tokens the chat streams use, and app connections made from them never come back. The cut-off is held against when each session signed in, which every renewal keeps, and an app connection counts from when the person said yes. Turning it on ends those app connections; a refresh of one is refused.
- **If the provider is down or misconfigured, nobody can sign in until it's fixed.** The page says so next to the choice and in the confirm before it turns on. The way back is outside the product: an owner contacts CoderHelm support, support verifies them out of band and turns only the rule off, with a note of who they verified and how. The rule goes off and that line goes in your audit log in one step, and every owner is emailed. Support never gets a session in your team from it. (A self-hosted install runs a recovery script with its own AWS credentials instead.)
- **Members who haven't linked yet** are listed, and turning the rule on warns with that list. CoderHelm never links anyone by email. While the rule is on, an admin gives each of them a **one-time link** from that list: it works once, for 72 hours, never for an owner, only through the team's provider and only when the provider's verified email is exactly the member's. It is accepted in one step only while the member is still unlinked with the same email and role, and on a verified, non-failing domain. The owners are emailed when a link is made and when it is used, and both go in the log. An unused link can be revoked.
- **New members** join with an admin's invite: they sign in with the team's provider with the invited email, and when its verified email is exactly the invited one (`ann+work@example.com` and `ann@example.com` are different addresses here) at a domain your team verified, CoderHelm makes their account and links it in one step. It never takes over an existing account.
- **Members can't unlink** the provider in use while the rule is on: they couldn't sign in again.
- **Email domains stay proven for a while.** While the rule is on, a TXT record that disappears doesn't release its domain at once: people who already linked keep signing in for 7 days, the domain shows as failing with the date it will be released, and the owners get an email with that date. No new member is accepted at a failing domain. Add the record back and choose **Verify domains**.
- **The setup is locked**: while the rule names a provider, that provider can't be turned off, removed, moved to another Okta or tenant, or lose a domain. Turn the rule off first. A new Okta app needs the owner's own **Test sign-in** with it within the last hour.
- **Keys follow their maker.** An API key or knowledge key works only while the member who made it is on the team and linked to the provider. Others are suspended (not deleted) and work again once their maker links; the confirm before turning the rule on names them (or says when the keys couldn't be read).
- **CoderHelm support** can open a support view of the team only with the support access your team grants and only from a session of theirs that passed a second factor. It is read-only and every page they read goes in your audit log.
- **Turning it off** brings the other ways in back at once. Turning it on or off goes in the log under **Settings, Roles**.

On a self-hosted install the rule is the organization's and works the same way. There are no DNS proofs there; it rests on the owner being the only one who can set up the provider and turn the rule on.

## Your second factor

Anyone can turn on two-factor sign-in for themselves in **Settings, Security**. Each person has **one** second factor, and CoderHelm asks for it however they sign in, whether or not a team requires it:

- **People who sign in with a password** use the password account's own codes: the sign-in asks for a code from your authenticator app (any TOTP app: 1Password, Google Authenticator, Authy and so on) after your password.
- **People who sign in with Google, GitHub, Microsoft or Okta** don't pass through that step, so CoderHelm asks for its own authenticator-app code right after them. The secret is encrypted with AWS KMS.
- **A code set up after Google or GitHub** is asked for after a password sign-in too.
- **A code on the password account** can only be checked at the password sign-in: signing in with Google or GitHub sends you back to sign in with your email and password.
- **Neither kind can be set up while you have one.** To change it, turn the current one off first: the app code takes a current code to turn off, and the password account's code can be turned off only from a session that signed in with it.

Your second factor is yours, not a team's: the same one is asked for on every team you are on. Turning it off ends your other sessions on every team.

## Requiring it for a team

A team's admins and owners can require two-factor sign-in for that team: **Settings, Sign-in, Require two-factor sign-in for this team**.

- **Set it up for yourself first**, then sign out and sign in again with your code. The switch waits until your own session used a code, so you can't lock yourself out.
- **Settings, Sign-in shows how many people haven't set it up**, and who. When you turn it on, each of them gets an email with a link to set it up.
- **With it on, someone without a second factor in this session sees only a setup screen** in this team: they can set one up (if they have none), sign out, or read why. Nothing else in the team works until they finish. Sessions from before the switch are checked at their next request. The rule is read on every request.
- **The rule applies to the team you are acting in.** If you are on several teams, a team without the rule isn't held back by another team's rule. Your second factor stays on while any team you are on requires it.
- **API keys** are not people and keep working: a key never signs in. **Settings, Sign-in** and **Settings, API keys** mark each live key made before the rule was turned on, with whether the person who made it has a second factor now (or has left the team). Check those and revoke any you don't recognize.

Turning the rule on or off goes in the log under **Settings, Roles**.

## A lost phone

If someone loses their phone, an admin resets their two-factor sign-in in **Settings, Sign-in** after checking it's really them (a video call, say). The admin writes how they checked; the reset and the reason go in the log under **Settings, Roles** before anything changes.

- Their second factor is the same on every team, so it is reset everywhere, and their sessions on every team end.
- They get an email saying their two-factor sign-in was reset, so a reset they didn't ask for doesn't go unnoticed.
- They set it up again at their next sign-in (straight away, if a team they're on requires it).
- Only an owner can reset an admin's or another owner's. Nobody can reset their own.

## Wrong codes

Wrong codes count against the person, by email, across every way in. Ten wrong codes in 15 minutes pause sign-in with a code for 15 minutes; each pause after that is twice as long, up to a day, and the count starts over after a quiet day. One Google or GitHub sign-in allows five wrong codes before you start again from the sign-in screen.

## Sessions that end

Your existing sessions end when an admin resets your two-factor sign-in, removes you from the team or changes your role, and when you turn your own two-factor sign-in off (the session you did it from keeps working). When the owner turns on Require single sign-on, your sessions on that team that didn't start with its provider end too.
